**The Last Horizon**

The ship *Endeavor* drifted silently through the endless expanse of space, its sleek hull reflecting the distant glow of stars, far too cold and distant to provide any real warmth. Commander Sarah Voss stood at the helm, her fingers grazing the console, feeling the quiet hum of the ship as it sped toward the unknown.

For years, humanity had sent missions to explore the reaches of the solar system, sending probes to Jupiter, Saturn, and beyond. But this mission was different. The *Endeavor* was the first human vessel to journey outside of the solar system, aiming for Proxima Centauri, a star just over four light-years away. It was a small step for mankind, but a giant leap for human knowledge. They had been planning this mission for decades.

Sarah, the commander, had always believed in the cause—exploring the stars, expanding the boundaries of human achievement. Yet, standing on the precipice of the unknown, something unsettled her. It wasn’t the silence of space. It wasn’t the vast, unyielding darkness that stretched infinitely in all directions. It was the realization that no matter how far they traveled, the answers they sought may not be what they expected. Or worse, there may be no answers at all.

The crew had long since grown accustomed to the monotony of space travel. Life aboard the *Endeavor* had become routine, almost too quiet. There were moments when Sarah would look out of the observation window, staring at the shimmering expanse, wondering if anyone, anywhere, would ever hear the hum of their journey. What if they were the only ones? What if humanity’s search for meaning in the universe was a fool’s quest?

The mission had been planned meticulously. They were equipped with the latest technology, advanced AI systems to ensure their survival, and decades of research. Yet despite the rigorous preparation, there had been no communication from Earth for over two years. The communication delays were expected—light itself took years to traverse the distance—but the silence felt oppressive, as though the universe was conspiring to close them off from everything familiar.

And now, they had reached the outer edges of Proxima Centauri’s system. It had taken years to get here, and Sarah’s pulse quickened as the final leg of their journey approached. A new world awaited, a planet in the habitable zone, an Earth-like exoplanet that could change the future of humankind. They would be the first to set foot on it. History would remember them.

Sarah stood, lost in thought, as the computer systems beeped softly. A soft chime echoed in the control room, pulling her back to reality. The planet was visible now, hanging like a jewel against the black velvet backdrop of space. The crew gathered around her, their faces lit by the glow of the screens. There was nervous excitement in the air—this was what they had worked for. This was the moment they had all been waiting for.

“Prepare for descent,” Sarah said, her voice steady. “We’re going in.”

The ship’s systems whirred as they adjusted course, slowly entering the planet’s atmosphere. The descent was smooth, but there was an undeniable tension in the air. Sarah’s heart raced as the surface of the planet grew closer, its details becoming more defined. Vast blue oceans. Landmasses, similar to Earth. It was perfect. Too perfect.

The *Endeavor* touched down with a soft thud, the landing gear absorbing the impact. The team wasted no time. They suited up in their exploration gear, their faces eager but cautious, aware of the weight of the moment. They opened the airlock and stepped into the unknown.

The atmosphere outside was breathable, the air rich with oxygen. The ground felt solid beneath their boots, and the landscape seemed eerily familiar. There were plants—tall, alien trees that swayed gently in the wind. Strange animals darted in the distance, their movements swift and deliberate. But there was no sign of intelligent life.

The team began to take samples, analyzing the atmosphere, the soil, the water. But Sarah’s mind kept returning to one thought: this world was too familiar. Too *right*. It wasn’t just similar to Earth—it *felt* like Earth.

As the day wore on, the crew set up their research station. Sarah wandered further from the group, her footsteps taking her into a dense forest-like region. Her mind was clouded with doubt. Was this truly a discovery, or had they merely found a mirror of their own world? She stopped in her tracks when she noticed something strange—an object half-buried in the soil.

Kneeling, she brushed away the dirt, revealing a small, worn metal artifact. A piece of technology. Her breath caught in her throat as she inspected it. It was unlike anything human technology had ever produced.

Her heart began to pound. She recognized the symbol etched into the metal. It was the same symbol that adorned the flag of Earth—the one humanity had used for centuries. 

But this was impossible. The artifact was far too advanced for anything humanity could have created. And yet, here it was, buried in the ground on this distant planet.

A sudden realization hit her, and her legs gave way beneath her. The twist of fate she had feared had come to pass.

She stumbled back to the ship, her mind racing. “Commander?” one of the crew members asked, concern in their voice. But Sarah barely heard them. She grabbed the communication console, her hands trembling.

"Earth, this is Commander Voss of the *Endeavor*. Do you copy?"

The response was immediate, crackling through the speaker. A familiar voice.

“Sarah… we’ve been waiting for you. Welcome home.”

She froze, her heart stopping. The voice was hers.

The twist of the journey was not the discovery of a new world, but the realization that they had never left Earth at all. The *Endeavor* was no spacecraft, no exploration vehicle—it was a simulation. A meticulously designed illusion, meant to make them believe they were venturing into the stars.

The world outside was a replica. The plants, the animals, even the distant stars had been crafted, simulated. Humanity had created an artificial world, a perfect illusion of exploration, to hide a terrifying truth.

There was no other life out there. The human race had already reached the last horizon—only it was not the stars. It was their own fate, and this was all that was left to them.
